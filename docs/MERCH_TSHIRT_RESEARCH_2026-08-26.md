# Merch T-shirt sourcing research: 50 pieces, mixed sizes

**Researched:** 2026-08-26
**Market assumption:** delivery to Germany, with EU resale possible
**Order assumption:** 50 adult T-shirts, one shared design, one delivery address, mixed sizes, no individual names/numbers, one front print unless stated otherwise.

## Executive recommendation

For a first run of 50 shirts:

1. **Use screen printing if the design is one to three flat colours and the same design goes on every shirt.** It is the strongest cost/quality fit at this quantity.
2. **Get quotes from Shirtigo Grossauflagen, WIRmachenDRUCK, and TeamShirts.** Shirtigo and WIRmachenDRUCK are the price candidates on the published benchmarks, while TeamShirts exposes a clearer 50-piece screen-print workflow and offers free standard shipping, size-collection tools, and sample ordering.
3. **Use Printful DTG/DTFlex when the artwork has gradients, photography, many colours, or when speed and flexible ordering matter more than the lowest landed cost.**
4. **Do not commit to a public “from” price.** Request a written all-in quote for the exact size/color matrix and print placement, then approve a sample before ordering all 50.

My practical choice would be:

- **Simple logo or graphic:** Shirtigo or TeamShirts, one-color screen print, one reliable unisex cotton blank.
- **Full-color game artwork:** Printful DTG/DTFlex or a local digital-transfer printer.
- **Event date inside two weeks:** prioritize the supplier that confirms the date in writing; current published lead times make this a real risk.

Because no delivery city was provided, this report names Germany-wide online suppliers rather than a specific local print shop. If pickup or face-to-face proofing matters, add one printer in the actual city to the same RFQ round.

### What “mixed sizes” should mean in the order

Keep the **same blank model and cut** across the run if possible. A safe first matrix is:

```text
S      5
M     15
L     15
XL    10
XXL    4
3XL    1
Total 50
```

Replace that matrix with pre-order data. Do not assume that an M from one brand fits like an M from another. Shirtigo explicitly warns that textile sizes are not standardized and provides garment-specific measurement tables. TeamShirts also supports ordering unprinted sample garments before the bulk order.

## 2. How to print: method comparison

| Method | Use it when | Advantages | Risks/limits at 50 pieces |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Screen print** | One shared design, usually 1–3 flat colours | Strong, vivid, consistent result; setup cost is spread across the run; good for 50 identical designs. Pantone/special colours are possible with suitable suppliers. | Setup cost increases per colour and usually per additional print area; gradients, photographs, and individualized names are poor fits. The artwork and print size need to be fixed before production. |
| **DTG** (direct-to-garment) | Detailed multicolor artwork, gradients, photographs, cotton shirts | No screen setup; excellent colour/detail flexibility; soft print on high-cotton garments. | Per-piece cost stays higher as quantity rises; best on high-cotton textiles; colour matching is less predictable than Pantone screen printing. |
| **DTF/DTFlex or digital transfer** | Detailed graphics, mixed fabric types, or small precise marks | Strong color and broad fabric compatibility; useful when DTG is not suitable. | Transfer can feel more like a surface layer; supplier-specific wash performance and placement limits must be tested. |
| **Local screen printer** | You can visit, inspect samples, or collect the order | Direct communication, potential pickup, easier escalation, and often competitive pricing for one repeated design. | Quality and contract terms vary widely; a low headline price may omit setup, proof, packaging, VAT, or delivery. Requires an RFQ and physical sample. |

Shirtigo’s own method comparison says screen-print setup is expensive at small quantities but the unit cost falls as quantity increases; DTG has low setup effort and supports almost unlimited colours and gradients. TeamShirts similarly describes screen printing as suitable from 50 pieces, with up to five colours per print area on its standard workflow.

## 4. Supplier evaluation

The assessment below is a desk evaluation based on published supplier information, not a physical test order.

| Criterion | Weight | What to verify |
|---|---:|---|
| All-in cost | 30% | Gross/net price, VAT, setup per colour and placement, large-size surcharges, proof fees, shipping, and replacement policy. |
| Order fit | 20% | 50-piece minimum, mixed sizes/colors in one order, same design across the matrix, stock availability, and whether cuts can be mixed. |
| Print/design fit | 15% | Number of colours, gradients, dark-shirt underbase, maximum print size, Pantone matching, and file requirements. |
| Product quality | 15% | Blank brand/model, fabric weight, composition, size chart, shrinkage, stitching, wash instructions, and sample result. |
| Delivery certainty | 10% | Production days, shipping days, tracking, written deadline commitment, and what starts the production clock. |
| Issue handling | 10% | Inspection/reclamation window, replacement/refund terms, personalized-item return rules, and a named support contact. |

### Supplier-specific evaluation

- **TeamShirts — strongest general first quote.** Clear screen-print threshold, clear one-colour setup fee, free standard shipping, size collection, sample route, and a 30-day return/exchange promise published for its personalized products. The delivery estimate shown on a current product page needs attention before an event order.
- **Shirtigo — strongest price candidate.** It supports the exact 50-piece minimum, arbitrary size/color combinations, free Germany/Austria shipping, and a published production window. The main weakness is that the attractive public “from” price is for 100 white shirts, not the requested 50-piece mixed order; custom products also cannot generally be returned simply because they do not fit.
- **WIRmachenDRUCK — worth a competitive quote.** It offers 25-piece screen-printing, S–5XL Fruit of the Loom blanks, up to four print colors, and free German standard shipping. Its very low public “from” price is tied to a maximum quantity, so it should be treated as a lead rather than a committed cost.
- **Printful — strongest artwork flexibility.** No minimum, one-print product pricing, DTG/DTFlex choices, and fast published digital-print delivery. The main weaknesses are dynamic shipping, more variables in garment sourcing, and likely higher landed cost for a simple repeated design.
- **Spreadshirt — strongest fallback for low minimums and customer-facing flexibility.** European production, automatic discount from five products, and a 30-day exchange policy. The published 50-piece screen-print base price excludes print cost and is materially higher, so it should not be the first bulk quote. Spreadshirt and TeamShirts are both brands of Spread Group, so they are not fully independent supplier alternatives.
- **FLYERALARM/local printer — valuable control option.** Use when physical samples, pickup, or premium blanks matter. The supplier must win the comparison with a written all-in quote; a public product page without a 50-piece total is not enough evidence.

## 5. Risk register and controls

| Risk | Severity | Why it matters | Control before payment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wrong fit or size mix | **High** | Clothing sizes vary by brand and cut; unsold sizes become dead stock. | Publish the exact measurement chart, collect pre-orders, order unprinted samples in S/M/L/XL, and keep one blank model across the run. |
| Missed event/delivery date | **High** | Production starts only after artwork/proof/payment approval; current supplier dates can be weeks apart. | Set the needed arrival date, add 7 calendar days of buffer, get the date in writing, and use tracked/express shipping only when confirmed. |
| Hidden or dynamic price | **High** | “From” prices may assume 100 white pieces and one-color printing; large sizes and extra print areas can change the total. | Require a PDF/email quote showing 50 units, every size/color, print positions, color count, VAT, setup, delivery, and expiry date. |
| Print looks different from the screen | **High** | Monitor color is not a physical proof; dark garments may need a white underbase; DTG and screen printing render colors differently. | Approve a physical printed sample or a supplier proof; specify Pantone only when the supplier confirms it can match it. |
| Artwork not production-ready | **Medium** | Screen print needs separations; low-resolution artwork can become soft or require paid cleanup. | Supply vector artwork where possible; otherwise use 300 dpi at final size and ask for a preflight/proof. Keep a master SVG/PDF. |
| Stockout or substituted blank | **Medium** | A supplier can have the product page live while a specific size/color is unavailable. | Ask for live stock confirmation for the complete matrix and prohibit substitutions without written approval. |
| Defects or short shipment | **Medium** | One bad print or missing size affects the resale set. | Agree on inspection window and remedy; count every size on arrival; photograph defects before washing; reserve 1–2 replacement units if the event is critical. |
| Personalized-item return limits | **Medium** | A shirt that simply does not fit may not qualify for a return, especially for made-to-order production. | Treat sample sizing as mandatory; show measurements at point of sale; keep a small exchange reserve if selling to consumers. |
| EU product-information noncompliance | **High if reselling in the EU** | Textile composition and product-safety information are not optional when making apparel available to consumers. | Keep fiber composition, manufacturer/responsible-person details, product warnings where relevant, and supplier documentation with the SKU. |
| Trademark/artwork rights | **High** | A printer may print supplied artwork, but that does not make unauthorized logos, characters, or images legal to sell. | Confirm ownership/license for the Roll-Drauf logo, game art, fonts, and any third-party assets before ordering. |

## 6. Recommended process

1. **Lock the product:** one unisex blank, color, fabric weight, and size chart.
2. **Lock the quantity matrix:** use pre-orders if possible; otherwise use the example matrix only as a starting point.
3. **Prepare artwork:** vector PDF/SVG preferred; define final print width, position, and Pantone/CMYK intent.
4. **Send the same RFQ to Shirtigo, WIRmachenDRUCK, TeamShirts, one local printer, and—only if the artwork is detailed—Printful.**
5. **Order one blank sample and one printed sample.** Test fit, wash once according to the supplier’s instructions, and inspect the print.
6. **Approve the written quote and proof.** No production should start on a verbal promise or a screenshot of a “from” price.
7. **Receive and QC:** count each size/color, inspect print alignment, check seams and stains, and record defects before distribution.
8. **Store evidence:** final artwork, approved proof, invoice, supplier quote, size matrix, garment composition, and batch photos.

## 8. EU resale/compliance note

This section applies if the shirts are sold or otherwise made available to consumers in the EU, not merely used privately.

- [Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A32011R1007) requires textile products placed on the EU market to show their fiber composition, with labeling/marking that is durable, legible, visible, accessible, and securely attached where a label is used. The composition information must also be visible before an online purchase.
- The [General Product Safety Regulation (EU) 2023/988](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/LSU/?uri=CELEX%3A32023R0988) includes distance-sale information requirements such as manufacturer/representative details, a clear product description, and relevant warning or safety information.
- The European Commission’s [REACH restrictions page](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/sectors/chemicals/reach/restrictions_en) identifies restrictions on certain carcinogenic, mutagenic, or reprotoxic substances in clothing, textiles, and footwear. Ask suppliers for current compliance documentation rather than relying only on a marketing claim such as “organic” or “fair.”

This is an operational checklist, not legal advice. If Roll-Drauf will sell at scale, confirm the final label and online product page with a German/EU product-compliance professional.
